About me

Why I do this research

My route into research hasn’t been a straight line. I trained in microbiology in Nigeria, worked on field surveillance in low-resource settings, then moved into community-based digital health research with young people in Lagos.

Along the way I became a software engineer, building and testing the kind of systems many health interventions now run on. In 2025 I moved to the UK for an MSc in Healthcare Operational Management at the University of Warwick, and joined quality improvement and mental health research teams.

That mix shapes how I work. I’ve seen good tools fail because they didn’t fit people’s lives, cultures or workflows. So I start with the people affected, stay reflexive about my own position, and design studies that let them define what “working” means.

“An intervention is only as good as its fit with the lives of the people it’s for.”
